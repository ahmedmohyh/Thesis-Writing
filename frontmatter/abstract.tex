Maintaining flow is a key performance factor in competitive esports, yet autonomous systems that protect flow during gameplay remain largely unexplored. This thesis investigates how a physio-adaptive system can detect and support flow states in \textit{League of Legends} (LoL). A mixed-methods, sequential-triangulation design is used: semi-structured interviews with competitive players, game-design experts, and physiological-computing specialists produce ten grounded-theory requirements (AF-01 to AF-10); these requirements inform the design and implementation of a working prototype.

The machine-learning pipeline is trained and validated on two secondary datasets (BIRAFFE2 and Irshad/PhySF). Offline experiments show that ECG-derived heart-rate-variability features, after subject-level baseline correction, provide the strongest and most deployable signal, reaching subject-level AUCs up to 0.677 (MLP, median split) and 0.790 (SVM, extreme percentile). Multimodal ECG+EDA+webcam models improve only marginally over ECG-only baselines, while subject-specific resting physiology is identified as a major confound.

Based on these findings, the live prototype is implemented as a local Windows companion app that streams ECG from a Polar H10 chest belt and runs a hard-majority-vote ensemble of five ECG-only classifiers. Webcam recordings are retained for post-hoc reflection but are not used for real-time inference. The app integrates with the local League Client API to detect matches and capture in-game events, and it provides post-game feedback through a flow timeline, tilt/break recommendation, and replay-linked reflection prompts. Autonomous in-game interventions such as notification suppression are deferred to a future iteration, because the current signal quality and the need to preserve competitive integrity make post-game reflection the safer and more grounded first step.

The thesis contributes a requirements-grounded design for flow-support systems in competitive gaming, a reproducible ECG-only classification pipeline, and a deployable prototype that balances physiological inference with player agency and privacy.
