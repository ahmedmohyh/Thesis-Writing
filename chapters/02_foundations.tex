\subsection{Outline}
The heading structure is hierarchical. The section headings are defined in the style sheets by name as heading 1, heading 2, etc.

Please do \textbf{not} use a fourth-level heading.

\subsection{Figures and Tables}
Figures are \textbf{always} inserted centered, with a name and, if applicable, a source.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{images/ude_figure_example.png}
    \caption{Figure name (Source if applicable)}
    \label{fig:ude_figure_example}
\end{figure}

It is important to always \textbf{explain} figures in the text and to refer to the figures in the text (see Figure~\ref{fig:ude_figure_example}). This also applies to tables. In the case of third-party figures and tables, the original source must also be indicated (e.g., in brackets at the end of the title). Tables should also be centered and labeled (see Table~\ref{tab:table_1}).

Please ensure that the font, color and style of figures and tables are \textbf{consistent}.

\subsection{Content}

Your text should be divided into paragraphs using figures and tables. It should be noted that a paragraph captures a thematic idea, whereby the core statement is usually found at the beginning of the paragraph and, from this, is structured by further discussions within the paragraph.
